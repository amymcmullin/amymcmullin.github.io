## Abstract

Every trade is getting a robot, a camera or an AI model, and every one of those devices is an endpoint someone has to secure. This session shares how a public technical college in a rural agricultural community is building an entry-level cybersecurity pipeline around that reality, on a shoestring budget.

The model is simple. Career programs across campus, from culinary and construction to automotive and accounting, adopt AI hardware tied to their industry, and IT and cybersecurity students become each device's security team. Students inventory, network, harden and document robotic arms, machine-vision kits, single-board Linux computers and voice AI assistants. They red-team the assistants with a fixed 15-question adversarial test that scores grounding, fabrication, scope boundaries and over-refusal. They also work tiered SOC-analyst shifts where new and returning students triage, escalate, contain and hand off together.

Attendees will hear what worked and what broke, including a consumer robot kept running on a self-hosted server after its vendor's cloud shut down. They will leave with:

- a repeatable model for turning AI hardware into hands-on security training
- a lightweight method any team can use to evaluate an AI assistant before deploying it
- strategies for funding a lab through small grants and cross-program partnerships
- concrete ways practitioners and hiring managers can mentor, host guest labs and write entry-level job postings that don't screen out new talent

This session is for educators, workforce leaders, hiring managers and anyone building pathways into cybersecurity for learners the industry rarely recruits.
