Mission

The AI Innovation Lab gives iTECH students practical, hands-on experience with the AI tools and devices reshaping technical careers — not by watching demos, but by standing up, networking, programming, securing and troubleshooting real AI hardware. It starts in Computer Systems & IT. The goal is AI fluency for every iTECH graduate.

Physical AI Lab

The Stations

Every piece of equipment in the Lab is its own station. Teams rotate through them, and each station carries a sequenced set of self-guided labs — so a student who starts on the robotic arm and a student who starts on the Vector robot both build the same core IT skills: device onboarding, networking, Linux and Python, security, and documentation.

🦾 Active station

Hiwonder xArm 1S Robotic Arm

A programmable desktop robotic arm that brings AI out of the screen and into physical work. Computer Systems students build AI projects with real industrial applications — the same pick, sort, and move logic behind modern manufacturing cells and inventory control.

⬤ Manufacturing · Inventory · Automation
  • Pick-and-place and sorting tasks modeled on manufacturing and warehouse work
  • AI-driven projects: recognize an object, decide where it goes, move it there
  • Pairs with the Skill Boss Logistics trainer for inventory-control scenarios
  • Students treat the arm as an asset on the network — addressed, documented, secured
  • Project work doubles as showcase material for Lab open houses
🤖 Active station

Anki Vector Robot

When the manufacturer's cloud servers shut down in 2024, Vector lost its brain. Students bring it back — a real service-restoration job that runs from Raspberry Pi servers and packet captures to Python and large language models.

Lab sequence
  1. Stand Up Vector's Brain — self-host wire-pod on a Raspberry Pi, capture the traffic in Wireshark, write a restore SOP
  2. Perceive, Decide, Act — calibrate its sensors in Python, then deliberately fool them
  3. Give Vector an LLM personality — and test it for hallucination and prompt injection
  4. Capstone: Vector as the Lab's ambassador, greeting visitors and explaining the Lab
🧩 Lab sequence in build

MatataStudio Nous AI Robotics Set

A buildable AI robotics kit. Students start the way an IT department would with any new device: unbox it, assemble it, and document it as a managed asset before a single line of code runs.

Lab 1
  1. Unbox, assemble & document the robot as a managed IT device — self-guided, with student handouts and deliverables
📟 Active station

UNIHIKER M10

A single-board Linux computer with a built-in touchscreen and sensors. Students connect over USB networking, work in the Linux command line over SSH, and write Python that reads live sensor data.

Lab sequence
  1. Sensor dashboard — hardware ID, SSH, Linux CLI, Python, live sensor tests
  2. MQTT sensor network
  3. Lab network status monitor
  4. Computer vision project
  5. Cloud-connected AI assistant

Instructional Model

How a Rotation Works

The Lab runs on rotating stations. Each team works through a self-guided lab packet at one station, then resets it and moves to the next. Labs are designed so any team can pick up any station on any day.

1

Rotate In

Teams arrive at a station and pick up where its lab sequence says to — with safe stopping points built in, so rotations never strand a project mid-step.

2

Self-Guided Lab

A Canvas Student Annotation packet walks students through numbered, checkable steps, with instructor checkpoints along the way.

3

Evidence & Analysis

Deliverables are written for the next technician, not just the teacher. Analysis and failure documentation carry more weight than procedure.

4

Reset & Hand Off

A station reset checklist — credentials removed, device restored — leaves the station ready for the next team.

Scaffolded by design

Every station follows a scope and sequence. Students get step-by-step exposure to a task early, even before they fully understand it — and that exposure pays off when the concepts arrive.

Returning students lead

Veteran students step into lead-tech roles with their own constraints and responsibilities, rather than repeating work they've already mastered.

Built to run without us

Instructor guides, answer keys, rubrics, and substitute notes ship with every lab — complete enough that the Lab keeps running no matter who is in the room.
