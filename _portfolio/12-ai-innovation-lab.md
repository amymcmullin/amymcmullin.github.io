*Immokalee Technical College · Co-led with Omar Rodriguez · Funded by Champions for Learning classroom grants and DonorsChoose*

A station-based AI lab at Immokalee Technical College. Student teams rotate through stations, each built around one piece of equipment:

* AIPI Lite handheld AI devices
* Hiwonder xArm 1S robotic arm
* MatataStudio Nous AI Robotics Set
* Anki Vector robot
* UNIHIKER M10
* M5Stack Cardputer
* Miko Mini conversational AI robots

Projects focus on AI with real-world physical applications, such as manufacturing and inventory control. Student work doubles as showcase material for the Lab.

## The idea

The Lab isn't meant to belong to Computer Systems. The question Omar and I started from was what AI actually looks like inside each career field on our campus, and whether students in those programs could get their hands on it. In our director's words, the concept is "an open AI lab focused on incorporating AI tools" across career fields.

The rotating-station format comes from the [2016 FarmBot hackathon](/portfolio/itech-farmbot-hackathon/): equipment as stations, teams moving through them. It's the format that holds up when students arrive and leave on different schedules.

## Funding: Silicon Glades

"Silicon Glades" is the name I gave the series of grant proposals that fund the Lab's equipment, written across iTECH programs so each one asks for what that program could use.

In November 2026, Champions for Learning delivered all eight of the classroom grants that made it through their system. A ninth, for the construction program, never went through. It went missing from the portal and I most likely missed resubmitting it during the last-day rewrite of the full set. It goes back out next cycle.

The same week, a DonorsChoose project funded two Miko Mini conversational AI robots. Applied Cybersecurity and CSIT students will use them to study how voice assistants process speech and to run privacy audits on voice-activated devices.

*The grant proposals were drafted with AI assistance; the research behind them — scoping the equipment, talking with instructors across programs, and working out what each program could actually use — is mine, and took considerably longer than the drafting.*
