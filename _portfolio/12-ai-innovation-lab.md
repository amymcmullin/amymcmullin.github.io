*Immokalee Technical College · Funded by Champions for Learning classroom grants and DonorsChoose*

A station-based AI lab at Immokalee Technical College. Student teams rotate through stations, each built around one piece of equipment:

* AIPI Lite handheld AI devices
* Hiwonder xArm 1S robotic arm
* MatataStudio Nous AI Robotics Set
* Anki Vector robot
* UNIHIKER M10
* M5Stack Cardputer
* Miko Mini conversational AI robots

Projects focus on AI with real-world physical applications, such as manufacturing and inventory control. Student work doubles as showcase material for the Lab.

## The idea

The Lab started from a question asked at a March 2026 school board meeting: how do you use AI in your shop?

Most K–12 AI work treats AI as a study assistant or a way to make curriculum more engaging. That isn't what a technical college needs. Our students are training for jobs where AI is an industrial tool: it gives physical equipment eyes, a brain, and the ability to adapt. So the Lab is open to every program on campus, not just Computer Systems. In our director's words, it is "an open AI lab focused on incorporating AI tools" across career fields.

Two design choices follow from that:

* **Edge, not cloud.** Everything runs on local hardware on campus. Nothing goes to a data center, which keeps student data on site and lets students see the whole system rather than an API call.
* **Stations, not a course.** The rotating-station format comes from the [2016 FarmBot hackathon](/portfolio/itech-farmbot-hackathon/). Equipment becomes stations and teams move through them, which is the format that holds up when students arrive and leave on different schedules.

## Funding: Silicon Glades

"Silicon Glades" is the name I gave the series of grant proposals that fund the Lab's equipment, written across iTECH programs so each one asks for what that program could use.

Each program's instructor submitted their own proposal; I scoped the equipment with each instructor and prepared the drafts. In November 2026, Champions for Learning delivered all eight of the classroom grants that made it through their system:

| Program | Project | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| Professional Culinary Arts | The Smart Sous-Chef | Wireless temperature probes, a waste-auditing scale and a vision station for heat transfer, food safety compliance and kitchen margins |
| Accounting Operations | Automated Inventory Auditing | A TurtleBot 4 mobile robot scans inventory so students reconcile physical stock with the general ledger |
| Automotive Service Technology | ADAS Sensor Calibration | LiDAR workstations for calibrating collision-avoidance zones and auditing sensor blind spots |
| Heavy Equipment Service | Autonomous Machinery | An AWS DeepRacer fleet and edge hub for autonomous navigation and reinforcement learning |
| Machining & Advanced Manufacturing | Precision Robotics | ROBOTIS OMX arms taught by demonstration to pick, sort and tend the campus sorting line |
| Cosmetology | 3D Volumetric Imaging | A turntable capture station that turns mannequin work into 360° digital portfolios |

A ninth, for Building Construction (camera-based PPE and red-zone safety monitoring), never went through. It went missing from the portal and I most likely missed resubmitting it during the last-day rewrite of the full set. It goes back out next cycle.

The same week, a DonorsChoose project funded two Miko Mini conversational AI robots. Applied Cybersecurity and CSIT students will use them to study how voice assistants process speech and to run privacy audits on voice-activated devices.

*The grant proposals were drafted with AI assistance; the research behind them — scoping the equipment, talking with instructors across programs, and working out what each program could actually use — is mine, and took considerably longer than the drafting.*
