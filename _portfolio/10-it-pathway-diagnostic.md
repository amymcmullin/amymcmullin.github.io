## Why interest alone isn't enough

Once students have foundations down, they need somewhere to go next. Asking what they're interested in gets you half an answer — a student can want a specialization they will struggle in. Asking only where they show aptitude gets you the other half, and a student who succeeds at something they don't care about.

This tool measures both, then balances them. The goal is to put a student on a path where they are both motivated and likely to finish, and to map that path to certifications Florida's CAPE program will actually fund — so the roadmap works for the student and for the school at the same time.

## The instrument

A web-based assessment covering ten IT areas: support and hardware, programming, networking, security, database design, data analytics, cloud, virtualization, AI, and project management.

I chose the domains from what I can genuinely teach and support with my background and the program's resources. Most map to the CompTIA Tech+ objectives, with a few changed to fit what we actually offer.

That choice reflects how I route students. Many arrive wanting A+, but its two-exam, all-or-nothing structure is a real barrier for the students I teach. Starting with Tech+ gets a credential in hand and a foundation under them; from there a student can specialize into Network+, Security+, AWS or another track that suits where their interest and aptitude actually meet.

Results sort matching certifications by difficulty and colour-code them by adult and dual-enrollment eligibility.

*The tool was built with AI assistance against my specification. The domains, the interest-and-aptitude model, the CAPE mapping and the advising logic are mine.*
